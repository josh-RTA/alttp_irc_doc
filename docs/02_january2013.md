```
[9:25am] <Kryssstal> a hookshot dash is what instakills the puff
[9:25am] <Kryssstal> then you have to dash cancel to get sword out again
[9:25am] <Kryssstal> and have something like 15 frames of leeway for the whole fight
[9:25am] <Kryssstal> compared to that video
[9:25am] <Kryssstal> so if you do it even half a sec slower you'll get 1 cycle
[9:26am] <Kryssstal> it'll spin with no puffs even
```

There's a lot wrong here, even outside of the hookdash stuff. Arrghus floats towards Link three times, and with each time, he pulls a random number that helps him decide to move for somewhere between 64 and 128 frames. This means your window of getting it is sometimes quite tight--to the point that it might not even be realistically doable--but sometimes is quite lenient as well. To just blanket say "you have 15 frames of leeway" is bad knowledge.

One interesting aside to go into is that Krystal shares a lua script that will draw Link's X and Y positions on the emulator's display, which helps her communicate different coordinates to look at. She (or whoever wrote the script) decided to model these positions using decimal, and this was the way people communicated about coordinates for years and years. It was only a few versions into the Pinkus led Practice ROM that he made the executive decision to change coordinates to use base 16 (or hexadecimal). He did this because seeing that your position is divisible by 4, 8, or 16 (crucial for a lot of things in the game) is *much* more readily apparent when the number is in hexadecimal compared to the usual decimal.
